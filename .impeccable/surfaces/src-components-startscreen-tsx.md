Scope: the prerendered start screen, the landing prose and the FAQ. Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience: a curious first-timer with a webcam and no gear, plus a hobbyist producer judging whether this is a real instrument. Job: understand what this is and press one button. Constraints: prerendered in Node, no browser globals at module scope, all copy and claims fixed, Buy Me a Coffee widget stays.

## Direction contract

THESIS: DJ Hands is the front panel of an instrument from a 1980s studio. It refuses the centred glass hero card glowing on near-black, and it refuses 80s-as-costume — no neon grid, no sunset gradient, no chrome lettering.

OWN-WORLD: Warm-grey painted panel in the console/rack register, darker recessed wells, brushed trim, 19-inch rack geometry with real screws. One grotesk, silkscreened small-caps legends, tiny. Colour appears only as a function code on knob caps and button ranks — never as decoration, never as hierarchy. LED ladder meters and scribble strips are the state vocabulary.

STORY: The visitor recognises hardware before reading a word, learns the two hand legends off the panel itself, and presses the panel's own illuminated button.

FIRST VIEWPORT: One rack-mounted panel filling the frame. DJ HANDS silkscreened across the panel head at the largest scale on the page, on a plate flush inside the unit's own edge (the user overruled the earlier overhang: "make sure it is inside the boundaries"). Beneath it two legend blocks side by side, LEFT HAND and RIGHT HAND, each a numbered imperative in its own hand's function colour — the code taught before the camera is on. The four figures run as a channel row: big numeral, ruled leader, entry. The primary action is a full-width illuminated panel button on the bottom rail, not a pill.

FORM: 1980s studio console and synth front panel. User-pinned, which beats the roll. Translated from assigned direction 7 of my ordered list (concert programme / Fluxus event score), whose topology, controls, state vocabulary and ritual still bind: the movement list becomes the channel strip with scribble strips, the event score becomes the panel legend, the ruled spine becomes the rack rail. Three of its four raises carry over — palette law, one family with no second face, and a spine where position is a readout. The fourth, scale that leaves its frame, was withdrawn by the user on 2026-09-02; the head plate now stops at the chassis edge, and scale alone carries it. Seed key 1f370d22.
